This is a topic I've wanted to write for quite some time. It has become more apparent now that i've worked at 3 startups in my career.

As I've gained more experience in the startup scene in SF, an emerging trait I often see from the founders I met is how polar they are on their
opinions. This might come off to people as arrogant but I think founders should indeed be polar, this is what you are ultimately getting paid for - it is definitely what investors are betting their money on, your polar decisions.&#x20;

## What is a founder?

or more specifically, what comprises a **founder**? There are a lot of material and writing out there on what a *great* founder is, but I want to take a step back. What does it actually take to just be a minimal be a founder?

what discerns me, a founding engineer working at a startup from the founder?

I think a founder, at its most simple core, consist of these 3 main traits.

* a person who is convicted in their idea
* malleable enough to change their path on achieving that idea
* and brave enough to try

That is it. Raising money, hiring, coding, all of these are just steps to creating a company. What makes a **founder** is just 3 simple traits. If you have an idea that you believe can work, you are brave enough to try, and stubborn enough to iterate as the market gives you more information, then you are a founder.
